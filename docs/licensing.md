# CareQFlow Licensing

CareQFlow uses version-based licensing.

This document explains the licensing model in plain language. It is intended to help users, evaluators, security reviewers, organizations, and contributors understand how CareQFlow may be used.

The authoritative licensing terms are defined in the repository's [`LICENSE`](../LICENSE) file and the license texts under [`LICENSES/`](../LICENSES/). If this document conflicts with the applicable license terms, the applicable license terms control.

## Version Licensing

### CareQueue 0.4.x and Earlier

CareQueue versions `0.4.x` and earlier were released under the MIT License.

The MIT License applicable to those releases is preserved at:

[`LICENSES/MIT.txt`](../LICENSES/MIT.txt)

Those historical releases remain available under the terms under which they were originally distributed.

### CareQFlow 0.5.0 and Later

CareQFlow version `0.5.0` and later versions expressly released under the current licensing model use the Business Source License 1.1.

The Business Source License 1.1 text is available at:

[`LICENSES/BUSL-1.1.txt`](../LICENSES/BUSL-1.1.txt)

The CareQFlow-specific parameters are defined in the root [`LICENSE`](../LICENSE) file.

For current BSL releases:

- The Licensor is `BlessedCow`.
- The Additional Use Grant is `None`.
- The Change Date is four years after the first publicly available distribution of each specific version.
- The Change License is the GNU General Public License, version 3 or later.

CareQFlow releases that remain under the Business Source License are source-available and should not be described as Open Source before their applicable Change Date.

## What the Business Source License Allows

The Business Source License permits non-production use of CareQFlow versions that remain under the BSL.

Examples of non-production use include:

- Reading and reviewing the source code
- Security review and auditing
- Development and testing
- Evaluating CareQFlow before purchasing a commercial license
- Building and running development instances
- Testing integrations and deployment procedures
- Creating modifications for development or evaluation
- Educational or research use that is not production use

These examples are explanatory only. The applicable Business Source License terms remain authoritative.

## Production Use

Production use of a CareQFlow version that remains under the Business Source License requires a separate commercial license from the Licensor.

Production use generally means using CareQFlow as part of real operational activity rather than solely for development, testing, evaluation, or research.

Examples include:

- Using CareQFlow as part of an organization's day-to-day operations
- Using CareQFlow to manage real authorization, workflow, administrative, or operational records
- Deploying CareQFlow for staff to use as an operational system
- Hosting CareQFlow for another organization
- Providing CareQFlow as part of a paid or managed service
- Incorporating a current BSL version into another production product or service

The production-use distinction is not limited to healthcare data or protected health information. A deployment may still be production use even when it does not process PHI.

If there is uncertainty about whether a planned deployment requires a commercial license, contact the CareQFlow project before relying on the deployment in production.

## Commercial Licensing

Commercial production licenses may be offered separately by the Licensor.

A commercial license may grant production-use rights that are not granted by the Business Source License while a release remains under the BSL.

Commercial licensing may be structured for different use cases, including:

- Individual facilities or locations
- Multi-facility organizations
- Enterprise deployments
- Hosted or managed services
- OEM or embedded use
- Redistribution arrangements

The specific rights, limits, support terms, pricing, duration, and deployment scope of a commercial license are defined by the applicable commercial agreement.

Purchasing or receiving a commercial license does not transfer ownership of CareQFlow or its copyright unless an agreement expressly states otherwise.

## Source Availability and Auditing

CareQFlow's source code may remain publicly available even when a current release is licensed under the Business Source License.

Source availability supports activities such as:

- Security assessment
- Technical due diligence
- Compliance review
- Architecture review
- Verification of privacy and security controls
- Evaluation of upgrade, backup, recovery, and rollback behavior

Public source availability does not by itself grant production-use rights beyond those provided by the applicable license.

## The Change Date

Each BSL-licensed CareQFlow version has its own Change Date.

For CareQFlow, the Change Date is four years after the first publicly available distribution of that specific version.

When a version reaches its Change Date, that version becomes available under the Change License specified in the root `LICENSE` file.

The current Change License is:

GNU General Public License, version 3 or later.

A newer CareQFlow release may still remain under the Business Source License even after an older release has reached its Change Date.

For example, an older release may have converted to GPLv3-or-later while a newer release remains under the BSL until its own Change Date.

## Historical MIT Releases

Changing the licensing model beginning with CareQFlow `0.5.0` does not revoke the MIT License from versions that were previously released under MIT.

Users who received CareQueue `0.4.x` or earlier under the MIT License retain the rights granted by that license for those versions.

The newer licensing model applies only to versions expressly released under the newer terms.

## Modifications and Redistribution

The Business Source License governs copying, modification, redistribution, and use of BSL-licensed CareQFlow releases.

Do not assume that because the source is publicly visible it may be used, redistributed, hosted, or incorporated into a production service without restriction.

Review the applicable license terms before redistributing or deploying modified versions.

A separate commercial agreement may grant additional rights when required.

## Contributions

Contribution licensing is separate from commercial production licensing.

See [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](../CONTRIBUTING.md) for current contribution requirements.

CareQFlow may require a Contributor License Agreement or other contributor terms before accepting substantive source-code contributions. This helps preserve clear copyright ownership and the project's ability to distribute, commercially license, relicense, or transfer CareQFlow while respecting contributor rights.

## Third-Party Software

CareQFlow may include or depend on third-party software distributed under separate licenses.

The CareQFlow license does not replace or override the licenses of third-party components.

Users and distributors are responsible for complying with applicable third-party license terms.

## Licensing Inquiries

For commercial licensing questions, use the contact methods published by the CareQFlow project.

Do not include confidential information, credentials, patient information, protected health information, or other sensitive data in public licensing inquiries.
